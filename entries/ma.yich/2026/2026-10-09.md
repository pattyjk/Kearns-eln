# Testing Microplastics Stock for Potential Contamination

- **Date performed:** 2026-10-09
- **Recorded by:** Philip Ma (ma.yich@northeastern.edu)
- **Project:** Effect of microplastics on amphibian's defense against Bd
- **Worked with:** Pranav Garge

## Question

Is the microplastics (MP) stock, kept by Prof. Kearns, usable for research?

## Hypothesis

Uncontaminated MP stock should not cause growth of microorganisms in a tryptone broth sample after inoculation, as there are no microorganisms present in the MP.

## Prediction

If hypothesis is right, the tryptone broth should not have a turbid appearance days after MPs were inoculated into it.

## Materials and reagents

1% Tryptone broth, in bottle
1x 50 mL tube
1x inoculation loop, in sterile package
MP stock, stored in "Specimen" bag, kept by Prof. Kearns
BSC
70% Ethanol
Sharpie, in BSC

## Procedure

1. Wipe off working surface in BSC with 70% Ethanol

2. Wipe off 1% tryptone bottle, 50mL tube, inoculation loop, bag containing MP stock before placing in BSC. Wipe off gloved hands with Ethanol before work in BSC.

3. Aliquot tryptone into 50mL tube. Using sharpie, label tube as "Tryptone + MP", with today's date, initials, and project name (MP)

4. Using inoculation loop, inoculate MPs into tryptone in 50mL tube. Take enough MP to cover the loop. Briefly swirl loop in tryptone, then close and invert tube.

5. Store 50mL tube in room temperature.

6. Remove everything from BSC, wipe BSC surface with ethanol, turn on UV light

7. Return in a few days to check on 50mL tube

## Deviations from the protocol

None, this was a rather simple procedure.

## Observations and results

Inoculated on Friday 10/9, checked on broth on Monday 10/12, no turbidity. No change in appearance for the broth. This results in MP stock usable and not contaminated.

## Interpretation

Yes, our results supported our prediction.

## Next steps

Start testing MPs on bacterial growth.
